What the sample can and cannot carry

The power calculation is the part that was missing, and it is decisive. At 58 clicks against 63, this test reaches 80% power only if the other books' true conversion rate is below 0.62% or above 33.9%. Anything in between is invisible to it. Its power to detect a true halving of the flagship's rate, from 12.07% to 6.03%, is 17.3%.

So the honest statement is not "the other books convert as well". It is "we cannot tell, and we also could not have told if they converted half as well". Those are different sentences and only one of them can fund a decision.

Three things I checked that did not rescue it, and one that made it worse:

  • Halo is dead as an explanation. All 25 lifetime orders are same-SKU purchases of the advertised book. product_days reports 25 orders, 25 same-SKU, 0 halo, everywhere. The click is not quietly converting into a flagship sale.
  • Every wider cut still favours the flagship. All US lanes lifetime: 15 of 164 against 10 of 136. All markets: 15 of 185 (8.11%) against 10 of 231 (4.33%). Not one specification comes out the other way, which is weak evidence that "indistinguishable" is being read as "equal" when it should not be.
  • The counts are provisional. Orders on 08-08 onward are floors pending Amazon's day-7 and day-28 restatements, and the non-flagship arm is the one more exposed. Every number here can still move.
  • Break-even sits inside the confidence band. At the observed non-flagship cost per click, break-even conversion is 12.8% on a $2.39 book and 7.3% on a $4.19 book. Both fall inside 3.58% to 19.59%. The data is simultaneously compatible with "these books are fine" and "these books lose money on every click".

Two hard limits on this lever

Print titles and subtitles are locked after publish. Tested against the live KDP form on 2026-08-09, not assumed. So the 3.7x is available only on the 33 Kindle titles, whose fields are editable, and on books not yet published. All 56 live print books are shut out of it without a new edition.

Click-through is not a term in profit per click. Profit per click is conversion times KDP-read royalty, minus cost per click. Raising click-through buys the same 100 clicks for the same $35; it only changes whether that takes four weeks or twenty-two. It shortens the calendar. It does not make a losing book profitable.

Which is why the real answer to "their handicap is CTR" is: partly, and the part that matters is that it makes them unmeasurable, not unprofitable. Twenty-three of the survivors' 107 clicks last week fell outside the space, jungle and ocean titles, spread across the rest of the delivering catalogue. Any single one of those books needs on the order of 115 clicks to settle its own conversion rate, which is the exact-binomial requirement for 80% power to show 7% beating a 2% floor. At roughly one click a week, that is not a plan. It is why the concentration question matters more than the creative one.
